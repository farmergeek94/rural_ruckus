# Architecture

How the code is organised. `AGENTS.md` holds the short rules; this document holds the
detail.

## Vertical slices

The game is organised by **feature, not by technical layer**. Each slice is one module
under `src/` and owns everything about its feature: plugin, components, resources,
systems, UI and tuning values. A change to a feature should touch one slice. Do not make
layer modules (`components`, `systems`, `resources`, `hud`): a slice that wants something
on screen spawns its own UI, as `truck/speedometer.rs` does. `src/ui/` is the front end by
itself, not such a layer, and no slice puts its screens there.

| Slice | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| `src/truck/` | The cast-wheel vehicle: a tire-shaped cylinder swept down from each mount, plus a wheel collider on the body at each hub that touches everything but the ground, so that trucks bump and climb over each other. its data from the `content` crate (`TruckConfig`: tuning and wheel positions; `TruckData`: a truck as plain data, with `in_color` for the built-in boxes, and its `Dashboard`), `input`, `spawn`, `looks` (meshes, textures, materials, and animated textures switched on the game clock), `axle` (drawn axles, and the bars, shocks and driveshaft that join them to the body, all following their wheels), `gearbox` (three forward gears, neutral and a reverse, automatic or, on the player's truck, manual by `Transmission`: it chooses the gear, runs the shift timer and decides how the clutch couples the engine to the wheels, as a torque converter or a driver's clutch), `engine` (the engine and drivetrain, simulated each physics step as plain data: a rotating mass with a torque curve, a blended friction that is the engine braking, idle, top and all-speed governors, a turbo whose boost lags, and a clutch that slips within its capacity and locks the engine to the wheels, which stalls under a manual driver; calibrated to `TruckConfig::engine_force` and `top_speed`, and coupled to the chassis speed, not to each wheel; `Engine` is what the dashboard, the speedometer and `sound` read, and `Engine::wheel_force` what `drive` pushes each wheel with), `drive` (suspension and tire forces, and the tip guard that eases the steering and brakes when an inside wheel lifts, on the computer's trucks only), `interpolate` (the drawn truck, between physics steps), `reset` (flip upright, and the `PlaceTruck` message), `wrap` (on a world that repeats, `RepeatingWorld`, which `race` fills in from the track: a truck that goes over an edge of the map is moved across to the other after the physics step, with the poses it is drawn between, and `TruckWrapped` tells the camera), `speedometer`, `lamps` (each `TruckData::lamps` lamp on the drawn truck: its picture as a glow and its beam as a spot light, shown while `TruckLamps::lit`; beacons turn and blink). A truck with `Held` has its brakes on and its throttle ignored. Holds the truck to drive in `ChosenTruck`, the computer's trucks in `ComputerTrucks`; the player's gets `PlayerTruck`. `GroundGrip` is what the ground under each tire multiplies its grip by; the slice leaves it at 1, and `footing` writes it. |
| `src/track/` | The track as data and the ground built from it: its data from the `content` crate (`TrackData`, `Gate`, `GroundTextures`, `Scenery`, `Footing`, `HeightGrid`, `Course`, and `settle`: how far each fixed object is lowered so that its foot touches sloping ground, found at load), `generator` (the built-in track), `ground_boxes` (the solid blocks of bridges and tunnel roofs, drawn and solid as part of the ground), `mesh` (shaded or textured terrain), `shading` (a normal map of a smooth surface through the heights, which the ground's shader lights by; shape and physics unchanged), `blend` (a map of the ground's cells, by which the shader fades the tiles of two cells into each other across a narrow band on their shared edge), `tile_material` and `tiles.wgsl` (the mipmapped texture array of tiles and the material extension that samples it, shared with `scenery` and `backdrop`; an unlit material shows the tiles' own colours; a vertex's second UV channel names its tile and animated texture, and `TileCycles` says which frame each is on; `tiles_prepass.wgsl` cuts a masked material's holes out of its shadows), `collider`. Holds the current track in the `Track` resource. An MTM2 track's ground repeats (`HeightGrid::repeating`): heights, footing and the course read the same a map's width over, `HeightGrid::offset` and `Course::offset` give the short way between two places, and the course joins its points the short way across an edge. The terrain's collider and the ground boxes are copied whole round the map, sharing their shapes; what is drawn of them as far as `DRAWN_PAST_EDGE` (1 km, the camera's far plane), each copy with a `VisibilityRange` (Bevy does not cull by distance). |
| `src/backdrop.rs` | The models round a track's horizon that its track file names (`TrackData::backdrop`), in their own texture array, each texture through its own palette, without mipmaps (only ever magnified). Kept centred on the camera every frame and scaled to just inside its far plane, unlit, drawn from both sides, with the weather's fog, darkened with the sun (`environment::Sun`). `BackdropSettings::on` (the options screen, `--no-backdrop`) hides it: its low-resolution art looks poor on a large screen. Drawn only, and only when the app can draw. Rules in `docs/formats/model.md`. |
| `src/scenery/` | The objects round the track: one mesh per model, one texture array for all, an opaque material for the models without holes and a masked one for the rest, and triangle-mesh colliders for the solid ones. `motion` (loose and moving objects) and `interpolate` (what is drawn of them). `animation`: animated textures, by writing which frame each is on into `TileCycles` when it changes; and keyframed models, moved in a straight line from one frame to the next on the game clock so that every copy moves in step. A keyframed mesh goes to the GPU once, with every keyframe after the first as a morph target; the CPU writes only the morph weights, and every copy has one bounding box round all its keyframes. Solid as its first frame. `facing`: type 8 objects turn about Y to the race camera after it moves. Drawn only when the app can draw. |
| `src/water/` | One level surface at `TrackData::water_level` over the whole track. `forces`: each truck is four columns, one over each wheel; the part under water is lifted (`BUOYANCY`) and held back (`LINEAR_DRAG`, `QUADRATIC_DRAG`). A truck drives through water and along the bottom when under; the water never stops it or puts it back. Added after `truck::TruckSystems::Drive`. `surface` and `water.wgsl`: a standard material with its own vertex and fragment shader. A swell rolls downwind on a patch round the camera (`PATCH_SIZE`, vertices 1.5 m apart), which the shader moves under the camera on a fixed grid; a finer patch ahead of the camera, round the truck it follows (`FINE_SIZE`, 0.25 m apart, its edge on the coarse patch's grid and its surface eased to the coarse patch's there), shows the bow wave and the wake as a shape; a flat sheet of 20 m squares covers the rest, and one test per pixel decides which of the three draws it (with MSAA, triangle edges left a dashed seam). A chop raised by the wind, gusts as ruffled patches, whitecaps in strong gusts. Trucks meet the water at its still level: the swell is only the look. It mirrors `ClearColor` at a glancing angle. `wind`: one direction, gusting as a pure function of time. `field` and `field.wgsl`: the water round the camera as a field of heights that the trucks disturb, stepped on the graphics card by a compute shader (`TEXELS` texels `SPACING` metres apart, addressed round and round so that the window follows the camera for nothing; `STEP` seconds a step, up to `MOST_STEPS` a frame): the wave equation at the speed of shallow-water waves up to `RIPPLE_SPEED`, held still on land so that waves reflect off the shore, damped in the shallows; and a foam channel, churned up by the trucks and where the water is steep, which spreads and fades. Each frame the trucks' doings are a list of `Source`s (`Disturbances`, written by `wake`): a tire piles the water up ahead and draws it down behind (`push`) from the leading edge of the chord the water cuts across it (`pushing_front`: what is under the water is what pushes), the body's bumper too once the water is up to it (`body_source`), a plunging wheel pushes it down (`lift`) and bursts foam; foam is also churned where the surface is steep or rises fast (`BREAKING`, `TURBULENCE`). A tire counts as in the water up to the truck's roof (`ROOF_ABOVE_CHASSIS`). `water.wgsl` reads the field's `look` texture (height, slopes, foam) between texels: it raises the patch's vertices by the height, tilts the normal (`FIELD_SLOPE`), and whitens the water where its foam noise is under the foam (`FOAM_EDGE`), the noise swirling with time (`FOAM_SWIRL`), dark water left between the lumps (`FOAM_GAPS`), lit as lumps (`FOAM_BUMP`) and rough (`FOAM_ROUGHNESS`); it fades to nothing towards the window's edge. Shallow water is lighter and greener (`SHALLOW_COLOR`). With `WaterSettings::reflections` off the water is drawn blended in the transparent pass instead of solid in the transmissive one, which spares the copy of the picture (with MSAA, a resolve of the whole picture: measured in `docs/smoothness.md`); with `WaterSettings::shadows` off it is a `NotShadowReceiver`. Nothing is read back to the CPU. `ripples`: the CPU's own count of the newest `MAX_RIPPLES` rings the trucks started, for where they come to the shore. `wake`: where a tire breaks the surface; a wheel that comes down fast (`SPLASH_SPEED`) starts a ring, and a truck leaves a wake. Each tire in the water is a `TireInWater` message for `particles` (where, the hub, how fast it and its tread go, how far up the water comes). `Wind::swell` gives the swell as plain numbers (`SwellWave`) from the shore's rule, so that foam rides the surface. `shore`: a depth map every 2.5 m from the ground's heights; the shader draws foam at the edge, lines of surf over shallows, and foam where rings break. Near the camera a crest or ring that reaches the shore breaks on it, as a `Surf` message. It repeats the shader's swell and ring rules, and a test checks that they agree. Both messages are written in `WaterSystems::Waves`; `WaterSettings::splashes` turns them off and leaves the ripples. `ice`: in weather that freezes the water (`weather::Weather::freezes_water`, only snow), a slab of ice 1 m thick with its top at the water level, solid in the ground's collision group, spawned with each race and switched with the weather; the liquid surface, forces and splashes stop. The look and the messages only when the app can draw. The values are the game's own: MTM2's water is not measured. |
| `src/particles/` | What trucks and the water throw into the air, drawn only when the app can draw. `pool` (knows nothing of the game): one mesh of squares, one per slot. The CPU writes a slot once when a particle is thrown; `particles.wgsl` moves, turns, sizes and fades it on the GPU, from an exact answer for gravity and drag towards the pool's air (`Air`, given when the pool is made, gusting on the shader's clock). A particle can trail along a line (`Particle::trail`), draw one of a grid of pictures (`Motion::shapes`, `Particle::shape`), fade out near what is behind it from the race camera's depth prepass (`Motion::soft`; the water is in the transmissive pass, which the prepass leaves out), or lie on a surface moved by up to three sine waves (`Motion::lies_on`, `Waves`), sorted just behind the others. Where it lands is found on the CPU when it is thrown. As one entity each, particles would cost a transform, cull, extraction and sort each per frame. Brightness from `weather::ParticleLight`. `dirt`: a tire on the ground (`TOUCHING`), on loose ground (`track::TrackData::loose_at`), not in water, throws dust in dusty weather (`weather::Weather::dusty`) and clods otherwise, more the faster it goes, slides, or spins from slow. Clods leave low on the back of the tread (`fling`, `tread_speed`), tumble and end on the ground, with splatter; dust is many faint puffs drawn long back along the tire's path into the puff before, rising and fading, soft against the ground (`DUST_SOFT`). Four shapes at random (`SHAPES_ACROSS`), coloured by the ground tile, in three pools (`CLOD_SLOTS`, `SPLATTER_SLOTS`, `DUST_SLOTS`). `DirtSettings::on` turns it off. `spray`: from each `water::TireInWater`, froth round the front and sides of a tire through water (`FROTH_*`), white lumps that go along with it, rise and fall behind, and mist off its pushing front (`mist_rate`), which the wind carries; how much goes with how far up the tire the water comes (`spray_share`), and its reach with depth (`reach`). The water a tire pushes aside, and the foam it leaves, are in the water's surface (`water::field`). Sheets of water (a `sheet` module) and then fans of drops were tried for the bow wave and taken out: the sheets looked like curtains of plastic, the drops like beads; foam on the water reads as water. Foam and froth are clusters of round bubbles (`foam_opacity`). A wheel that comes down into the water throws a crown of froth from the rim of its footprint, out more than up (`splash_lumps`), foam patches all round that lie on the water and ride the swell (`water::Wind::swell`, or flat with `WaterSettings::flat`), mist over it, and a spreading ring. The drop pool is the shore's (`surf`). `surf`: from each `water::Surf`, spray where the water breaks on the shore. Drops, mist, foam and froth are four pools (`DROP_SLOTS`, `MIST_SLOTS`, `FOAM_SLOTS`, `FROTH_SLOTS`) with `water::Wind` as their air (foam at `FOAM_DRIFT` of it); mist soft (`MIST_SOFT`); drops and froth hardly (`DROP_SOFT`, `FROTH_SOFT`), since spray that faded wherever a tire was behind it was gone from round the tires. Drops are a streak as long as their path in `EXPOSURE` (0.04 s), end on the water or ground, and inside 6 m of the camera look smaller the nearer they come. |
| `src/footing.rs` | What the ground is under each tire. Each physics step, before `TruckSystems::Drive`, sets every truck's `truck::GroundGrip` from `TrackData::footing_at` under the bottom of each tire: ice grips `ICE_GRIP` of the tire's grip, loose ground all of it, firm ground a tenth more. In weather that freezes the water, a tire over the water near its level is on ice too. A POD track's footing comes from its `.TTY` (`docs/formats/texture_types.md`); the built-in track has none. `ICE_GRIP` is the game's own. |
| `src/sound/` | The sounds of the game: for now the trucks' engines, heard. The `truck` slice decides what an engine does (`truck::Engine`: its speed, its fuel, whether it runs); this slice decides what that sounds like. Every truck sounds the base game's one engine: its three engine loops (`loops`: idle, M1, M2), which the base game finds in `SOUND.POD` as the samples its engine files share (`base_game::BaseGame::engine_loops`). Each drawn truck (`truck::TruckVisual`) plays all three, and each frame the gear chooses one (`loop_for`: idle in neutral and standing, M1 pulling away in first or reverse, M2 in the other gears), faded in over `FADE_TIME`, and `Engine::revs` sets its speed (`loop_speed`); its loudness stays on the throttle. Silent without the base game. Heard from the race camera (`camera::ChaseCamera`), which it gives a `SpatialListener`; silent while the game's clock is paused, and while the engine is stalled. `SoundSettings` (the volume: the options screen). Only when the app has Bevy's `AudioPlugin`. Reads `truck::Engine`, `Gearbox`, `TruckInput` and `TruckVisual`. Uses `truck`, `camera` and `base_game`. |
| `src/weather/` | Clear (the default, as the game was), overcast, fog, rain, storm or snow, in `WeatherSettings`: F7 steps it in a race, or `random` picks one as each race begins, with the time of day. `conditions`: each weather as plain numbers (sky colour, sun and ambient brightness, visibility, what falls, grip, lightning). `sky`: `ClearColor`, the sun's (`environment::Sun`) brightness and colour, and `DistanceFog` and `AmbientLight` on the race camera, so that the showroom keeps its own light. A storm flashes with lightning. `fall` and `fall.wgsl`: rain or snow round the camera, one mesh of squares made once and moved by a vertex shader (on the CPU it cost 4 to 6 ms a frame in a dev build); each drop has its place in a box laid over the world like tiles, so the rain keeps to the world as the camera moves. `cover`: no drop under a bridge or roof. For each square metre round the camera, the height of the highest static body is found once by a ray cast down and kept in a ring the shader reads; only squares the camera has just come near are cast, at most `RAYS_PER_FRAME` a frame. `grip`: every truck's grip and handbrake grip are its dry grip (`DryGrip`) times the weather's share. `WeatherSettings::time_of_day`: day, dusk (a low sun, orange under a clear sky, grey otherwise) or night (dim blue moonlight), which F8 steps; dusk and night light the trucks' lamps. The grip works in any app; the look only when the app can draw. The values are the game's own. |
| `src/race/` | Laps and checkpoints: `gate` (crossing test), `progress` (the rules as a pure state machine, `position` and `standings`), `along` (how far each racer has still to drive to its next gate along the course, which `position` and `standings` compare between racers through as many gates; where each gate is along the course is found once a race, as the way that takes the gates round the course once; a racer is looked for only between its last gate and its next), `start` (the countdown: every racer held on the grid with `truck::Held` until GO, in ticks of the race clock, and again on a restart), `results` (once the player has finished, every truck's place, time and best lap, kept up to date; `keys::Control::RaceAgain` builds the whole race again), `systems` (progress tracking, the starting grid, the C key and its `BackToCheckpoint` message), `markers` (gate poles and banners), `hud` (race readout, with the player's position when there are others), `compass` (a dial whose needle points to the next checkpoint, turned against the drawn truck's heading), `map` (`keys::Control::Map` shows a map of the track in the bottom right corner: `track::map_image`, painted once as the race begins, with a dot per drawn truck placed by `track::map_frame`, red for the player's and blue with its place for each of the computer's), `pause` (Esc: `RacePause::Paused`, a state that exists only in a race, stops the virtual clock and shows a dialog to continue, restart the race (`GameState::Racing` left and entered again), save a screenshot to `screenshots/`, or cancel the race, which writes `RaceCancelled` for the front end, or quits when `RaceSettings::front_end` says there is none). |
| `src/opponents.rs` | The computer's drivers: one `ComputerDriver` on every truck that is not the player's, which writes that truck's `TruckInput` as the keys write the player's. `plan` is the rule, a pure function of the course and the truck's state. A driver keeps to its own line near the middle of the road, steering at a point a little way up the course. It catches up mostly on the straights: the further down the order and behind the leader, the more power its engine is given (`CHASING_POWER`, written to its `TruckConfig`), and a little more it asks of its tires in bends and the later it brakes (`chasing`, `Style`). Downhill it counts on less braking (`DOWNHILL_BRAKING`). Fixed solid scenery on the road counts as trucks stopped there (`Obstacle`): its outline at a truck's height within the course's width is found once a race (`obstacles_on`). A ramp (`SceneryObject::ramp`) is driven over. A driver sees what is in its way from 3 s before it would reach it (`LOOK_AHEAD`), never less than 45 m ahead (`LOOK_OUT`). One that comes up behind another truck moves its line aside (`passing_line`) and never slows for it; boxed in, it drives on until a side is clear. Only the road ahead sets its speed. A driver that gets nowhere for a few seconds, or passes its next checkpoint, asks the race to put it back. `OpponentsSettings::difficulty` chooses the course every driver follows (`TrackData::course` or one of `TrackData::other_courses`, whose left-out corners the importer puts back as curves) and scales its engine and catch-up power. Where each checkpoint is along that course is found once a race (`along_the_course`). Once the player has finished, a driver takes the player's truck too, with `truck::Autopilot` so that the keys leave it alone. |
| `src/camera/` | The chase camera, and `views`: V (`keys::Control::ChangeView`) changes to the cockpit, fixed to the drawn truck near the front of its body under its roof, with the player's truck hidden; the look keys and the right stick swing the chase camera round the truck, or turn the cockpit's eye, left, right or behind. A view is laid over what the rig placed, in `CameraSystems::Place`. On `truck::Autopilot` (after the finish) another view is picked at random every 7 seconds, with a random distance for the chase camera, a swing round the truck or a fade through black. `CameraView` says which view. `dashboard`: in the cockpit, the player's truck's `truck::Dashboard` as UI over the 3D view, stretched over the window and unlit: the picture for the way the player looks, and, looking ahead, the steering wheel picture and the dials' needles; with a dashboard the eye is tipped down so that the road is in its window. `chase`, `rig` and `spring` are the camera itself and know nothing of the game; `mod.rs` is the only code that knows both: it spawns the camera for a race, tells it each frame where the truck is drawn and how fast it goes (after the truck has been placed), and the height of the ground under where the eye wants to be. `CameraSettings`: antialiasing (off, FXAA, 4x MSAA), bloom (HDR, so that the lamps glow) and the rig's numbers. |
| `src/base_game.rs` | Not a slice: the base game's archives on disk (`--base-game=FOLDER`, else the `Shared` and language folders the player chose, else `base/`), as a resource round `pod::BaseGame`, which reads each archive's directory when it opens and a whole archive only when a file in it is first asked for. `main.rs` opens it and inserts it as a resource; `front_end` hands it to the loaders; `sound` asks it for the engine's loops. With `main.rs` and `front_end.rs`, the only code that names the `pod` crate. |
| `src/mipmaps.rs` | Not a slice: makes a texture's smaller copies on the CPU, in linear light, for `track` (ground and scenery tiles) and `truck` (paintwork), and plainly averaged for a truck's normal maps. |
| `src/frame_pacing.rs` | Not a slice: steps the game clock evenly under vsync, by the refresh interval (the mean of the last sixty frames) instead of each frame's own jittery measurement. A missed refresh is made up gently, a real stall passed on whole. `Pacer` is the rule, a pure function of frame lengths. Added by `main.rs`, only with vsync; tests drive time themselves. |
| `src/environment.rs` | Sky colour and sun of a clear day (`SKY`, `SUNLIGHT`), which `weather` changes. `EnvironmentSettings`: how many shadow cascades the sun has and how far they reach. |
| `src/display.rs` | The window. `DisplaySettings`: fullscreen, borderless or windowed, and vsync (off, on or strict). `main.rs` opens the window from it; a later change goes to the window and to `frame_pacing::FramePacing`. |
| `src/controls_help.rs` | On-screen key reference, from `keys::KeyBindings`. `ControlsHelpSettings` hides it. |
| `src/diagnostics.rs` | Off unless asked for. `--log-fps` prints once a second: frame rate, frame time, the shortest, median and longest frame and how many ran late, how many meshes passed culling, and how evenly the view turned. `--autopilot` drives the course by pressing the player's keys, as the quickest computer driver would on a clear road (`opponents::lead_driver`), so that problems that only show while driving can be reproduced. A diagnostic, not an opponent. |
| `src/physics_debug.rs` | F1 toggle for Avian's collider wireframes. Only in an app that can draw. |
| `src/graphics_debug.rs` | F2 toggle for a panel of the graphics settings that cost frame time (antialiasing, shadow cascades and distance, anisotropic filtering), each stepped by a key while the panel is open, with the frame time underneath. It only writes `CameraSettings`, `EnvironmentSettings` and `TrackSettings`; each slice carries the change to what it has spawned. |
| `src/front_end.rs` | The game side of the front end, and the only code that knows both the `ui` module and the slices. Lists the archives in `trucks/` and `tracks/` and the trucks and tracks in the base game's archives (`base_game`, ids `base:<archive>#<file>`), puts the highlighted truck on the turntable (`truck::TruckDisplay`), paints the highlighted track's map (`track::map_image`), turns the garage's dials into `truck::TruckSetup`. On GO it closes the front end, puts up the `ui` loading screen and, once it is on show, loads the track and trucks, hands them to the slices and enters `GameState::Racing`. The screen stays up, with the virtual clock stopped, until the race's frames are quick again (`lift_the_loading_screen`). `race::RaceCancelled` comes back; EXIT closes the game. Remembers choices in a `store::Store`. Asks for the base game's `Shared` folder and one language's folder when nothing says where they are (`FrontEndSettings::ask_for_base_game`), in the `ui` folder browser or from the options screen's two FILES lines; each is kept in the store (`BaseGameChoice`), opened as the new `BaseGame`, and the lists made again. The options screen: `OPTIONS` lists each line and the slice settings resource it sets (`display`, `camera`, `environment`, `track`, `controls_help`, `water`, `dirt`, `backdrop`, `weather`, `physics`, `truck::SpeedUnits`, `truck::TruckLooksSettings`, and `keys::KeyBindings`, on a CONTROLS page in sections). A change goes to that resource and to the store at once. Beside RESTORE DEFAULTS, the INTEGRATED GRAPHICS preset (`integrated_graphics`, `--integrated-graphics`) turns down what costs most on a built-in GPU. Added by `main.rs` only when the command line names nothing to race. |
| `src/keys.rs` | Not a slice: which key does each thing in a race (`Control`, `KeyBindings`), which every slice asks rather than naming a key, and the keys that may be bound (`BINDABLE`). The arrow keys always drive as well; Esc is not bindable. The front end's CONTROLS page binds them. |
| `src/sky.rs` | A picture on a dome round the camera, behind the backdrop, as large as fits inside the far plane, unlit and unfogged. The track's own sky (`TrackData::skies`, from the `.LVL`'s lines 11 and 12) in clear weather by day, the base game's cloudy one when overcast by day, its dusk one at a clear dusk, its night one at night, and none at an overcast dusk or in fog, rain, storm and snow. How MTM2 laid the picture is open (`docs/formats/level.md`). Drawn only when the app can draw. |
| `src/collision_groups.rs` | Not a slice: what may touch what, as Avian's collision layers. The ground is in `GROUND`, a truck's body in `TRUCK_BODIES`, its wheel colliders in `WHEELS` and their cores in `WHEEL_CORES`, which touch the ground and the bodies of other trucks alone. `track` and `truck` both use it. None is Avian's default layer, which every other collider is in. |
| `src/physics.rs` | Not a slice: how Avian is set up: its plugins, `truck::decide_tire_contacts` at the end of each narrow phase, and `PhysicsSettings`: steps a second (120) and substeps (6), which the options screen's PHYSICS lines lower. `main.rs` adds it, and so does every test app that runs physics. |
| `src/game_state.rs` | Not a slice: `GameState { FrontEnd, Racing }`, which every slice may use. Everything a race is made of is spawned on `OnEnter(GameState::Racing)` with `DespawnOnExit(GameState::Racing)`. |
| `src/ui/` | Not a slice: the front end's screens and showroom, and the loading screen (`LoadingScreen`). Knows nothing of the game: it shows the plain data it is handed (`Catalogue`, `Dials`, `Choices`, `Settings`, `TrackPreview`, `LoadingScreen`) and says what the player did (`TruckHighlighted`, `TrackHighlighted`, `GoPressed`). Its rules are a pure state machine (`model`), its look one `Theme`. Only `front_end.rs` may use it, by its full path (`crate::ui::`), so that it is not mistaken for Bevy's. |
| `src/shaders/` | Not a slice or a module: every WGSL shader, one file each. Each belongs to the slice that draws with it (`particles.wgsl` to `particles`, `water.wgsl` and `field.wgsl` to `water`, `fall.wgsl` to `weather`, `tiles.wgsl` and `tiles_prepass.wgsl` to `track`), which embeds it at `embedded://monster_truck_rural_ruckus/shaders/NAME.wgsl`. `embedded_asset!` takes only a path below the calling file's folder, so each slice puts its shader's bytes into Bevy's `EmbeddedAssetRegistry` directly, under its `SHADER_PATH` without the `embedded://`. |
| `src/store.rs` | Not a slice: what the game remembers between runs, by table and key, in one file on redb. No Bevy in it and no redb in its API. Only `front_end.rs` may use it. |

`src/lib.rs` only declares modules, and `src/main.rs` only configures the window and
physics and lists one plugin per slice. Where tests go is in "Tests" in
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

The repository is a workspace of three crates: the game at the root, its content model
in `crates/content`, and the Monster Truck Madness 2 loader in `crates/pod` (see
"Content pipeline"). Within the game, `ui`, `store` and `camera/chase` were once crates
too, which is why each keeps a hard edge: private submodules, few public names, nothing in
it names the game. Keep those edges, whatever the compiler now allows.

## Slice rules

- **One plugin per slice**, a `pub struct FooPlugin` in the slice's root, which registers
  every system of the slice. `main.rs` never registers a slice's systems itself.
- A slice starts as a single file. Turn it into a folder when it grows past one concern:
  `mod.rs` holds the module doc, the plugin, the marker component and the `pub use`
  re-exports, with one file per concern beside it.
- **The slice root is its public API.** Submodules are private (`mod`, not `pub mod`);
  what other slices may use is re-exported from the root. Systems and helpers are
  `pub(super)`. Other slices write `truck::Truck`, never `truck::spawn::...`. Keep the
  API small: mostly components and resources that other slices query, plus the plugin.
  Prefer components, resources and messages to calling another slice's functions.
- **Dependencies point one way.** `truck` and `track` depend on no other slice. To move a
  truck from another slice, write a `truck::PlaceTruck` message from a system ordered
  `.before(TruckSystems::Place)`. If two slices need each other, the shared piece is in
  the wrong place: move it to the slice that owns the data. Who uses whom:
  - `scenery`: `track`, `camera`.
  - `backdrop`: `track`, `camera`, `environment`.
  - `water`: `track`, `truck`, `weather`. Used by `particles` (its messages and wind).
  - `particles`: `track`, `truck`, `water`, `weather`.
  - `weather`: `environment`, `camera`, `track`, `truck` (it lights `truck::TruckLamps`).
    Used by `particles`, `water`, `footing`, `sky`.
  - `sky`: `track`, `camera`, `weather`.
  - `footing`: `track`, `truck`, `weather`.
  - `race`, `camera`: `truck`, `track`.
  - `sound`: `truck` (`Engine`, `Gearbox`, `TruckInput`, `TruckConfig`, `TruckVisual`,
    `TruckWheels`, `Wheel`), `camera` (the listener on `ChaseCamera`), `base_game`.
  - `opponents`: `truck`, `track`, `race`. Used by `diagnostics` (`--autopilot`) and
    `front_end` (difficulty).
  - `front_end`: `truck`, `track`, `race`, the settings resources of `camera`,
    `environment`, `display`, `controls_help`, `water`, `particles`, `backdrop`, `weather`
    and `sound`, and `opponents::OpponentsSettings`.
  - `graphics_debug`: `camera`, `environment`, `track`.
  - Nothing depends on `backdrop`, `water`, `particles`, `sound`, `sky`, `footing`,
    `front_end` or `graphics_debug` except as listed above and `front_end`'s options.
- Do not make a `common` or `utils` module. Duplicate a three-line helper rather than
  couple two slices. Extract shared code only when a third user appears, and name the
  module after what it is.
- A slice must work in a headless app (see the tests), so it must not assume a window, a
  renderer or another slice's plugin unless its module doc declares it.

## Content pipeline

```
.POD  ──►  pod::pod (parse)  ──►  pod::track, truck, sound (convert)  ──►  content (TrackData, TruckData, EngineLoops)  ──►  game
```

- **`crates/content`** (`content`) is the content model: `content::track::TrackData`,
  `content::truck::TruckData` (with its `TruckConfig`) and `content::sound::EngineLoops`,
  with `HeightGrid`, `Course`, `settle` and `mipmaps`. Plain data in the game's units and
  axes. Bevy's vectors, and the `Component` and `Resource` derives on `TruckConfig` and
  `TruckSetup`, are the only engine types in it. The `track`, `truck` and `sound` slices
  re-export its types, so the rest of the game writes `track::TrackData`.
- **`crates/pod`** (`pod`) reads a Monster Truck Madness 2 archive (its root: one
  module per file format, `std` only, no file system access, no panics on bad input,
  MTM2's own units and axes) and converts it (`pod::track`, `pod::truck`,
  `pod::sound`): feet to metres, a corner origin to a centred one, a flipped Z axis,
  headings to yaw. It depends on `content` and on nothing of the game. `pod::BaseGame`
  is the base game's archives on disk, which a track or truck borrows files from. The
  track and truck converters may not use each other, so the few lines that turn a model
  into a mesh are written out in both.
- **The game** is built against `content` alone. `main.rs`, `front_end.rs` and
  `base_game.rs` are the only files that name `pod`: they choose what to load and hand
  the result to the slices (`track::Track`, `truck::ChosenTruck`, chosen by inserting
  them before the plugins). The built-in truck is one `TruckData` and a POD truck
  another. Nothing downstream knows a track came from a POD.
- A loader for another source (an on-disk content pack in the project's own formats:
  glTF, PNG, RON) is another crate that depends on `content` and fills the same types.
  Do not build it until it is needed.
- Anything a source expresses that the model cannot hold yet is skipped. To add it,
  extend `content`; never make gameplay reach into a loader's types.
- **`src/ui/`** and **`src/store.rs`** know nothing of the game (see the table);
  `src/front_end.rs` translates.
- **`src/camera/chase.rs`**, with `rig` and `spring`, is the chase camera itself: Bevy
  alone, no trucks, tracks, physics or `GameState`. It is handed a `Target` (position,
  velocity, facing) each frame and places the camera. Its rules are a pure state machine
  (`rig`) over an exactly solved spring (`spring`); `tests/camera_smoothness.rs` drives
  them along scripted paths at even and uneven frame lengths, with a budget for every
  figure. `src/camera/mod.rs` translates.

## Gameplay is built against data

Tracks come from the built-in generator and from PODs, and gameplay must not be able to
tell them apart:

- A track is plain data, `track::TrackData` (heights, course, checkpoint gates, start
  position), which systems turn into entities. `track/generator.rs` makes one instance,
  `pod::track` another. Keep `TrackData` free of entities, handles and other
  engine types.
- Nothing outside `track/generator.rs` may know that the built-in track is procedural.
  Ask the `Track` resource (`track.heights.height_at(x, z)`).
- Checkpoint and lap logic depends only on that data and on truck positions. MTM2
  checkpoints are gates crossed in order: model them that way, not as "reach this point"
  spheres.
